Image Restoration: Results and Conclusion

Gaussian noise and salt-and-pepper noise were added to the original photograph to evaluate image restoration techniques. 
Gaussian Blur and Median Blur were applied to both noisy images, and the restored outputs were evaluated using Peak 
Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) and Structural Similarity Index (SSIM).

For Gaussian noise, Median Blur achieved a PSNR of 31.61 dB and an SSIM of 0.7685, slightly outperforming Gaussian Blur, 
which achieved 30.55 dB and 0.7486, respectively. For salt-and-pepper noise, Median Blur achieved the best result, with a 
PSNR of 40.64 dB and an SSIM of 0.9744. Gaussian Blur performed less effectively on this noise type, with a PSNR of 29.39 dB 
and an SSIM of 0.7022.

The experiment demonstrates that Median Blur is particularly effective for removing salt-and-pepper noise while preserving the 
original image structure. The results also show that the effectiveness of a filtering technique depends on the type of noise 
present in the image.
